5 Signs Your Company Actually Needs a Chief of Staff

FOR FOUNDERS & CEOS 7 min read
Abraham Otieno

Most founders hire a Chief of Staff either too early, and bore them, or too late, after months of avoidable chaos. Here are five concrete signs you've actually reached the point where a Chief of Staff pays for itself, and what to do if you're not there yet.

1. Decisions get made in meetings, then vanish

If your leadership team agrees on things that never actually happen, because nobody captured the decision, named an owner, or chased it, that's the single clearest signal. A Chief of Staff turns meetings into tracked commitments instead of good intentions.

2. Strategic projects stall between departments

A market entry, a systems migration, a partnership: initiatives that touch multiple teams and quietly drift for months because no single person owns the cross-functional execution. This is exactly the gap a Chief of Staff closes.

3. You're the bottleneck on everything cross-team

If every decision that spans two departments has to route through you personally, you've become the company's integration layer by default. A Chief of Staff absorbs that load so the company keeps moving without you standing in the middle of every handoff.

4. Board prep is a recurring panic

Assembling the board pack the night before, every single quarter, is a sign the operating rhythm itself is missing, not just that you're busy. A Chief of Staff runs board and investor operations on a calendar, not in a scramble.

5. Your leadership team has outgrown its rhythm

Once you have four or more people in leadership, informal coordination stops working on its own. The cadence, weekly meetings, goal check-ins, a real decision log, becomes a job in itself. Usually nobody's actually doing that job; it's just happening badly by default.

If you only see one or two of these

You probably don't need a full Chief of Staff yet. If the pain is specifically your calendar and inbox, hire an excellent Executive Assistant, that's a different and cheaper fix for a different problem. If it's three or more of the signs above, that's when the case for a Chief of Staff, fractional or full time, actually holds up.
